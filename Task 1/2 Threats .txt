Threat 1 — Piracy and intensifying competition erode Webflix's negotiating position and pricing power.
The Company competes against a widening field of entertainment video providers, including services that distribute pirated content, and management explicitly identifies piracy as a threat that is "subject to rapid global growth" because its core proposition — free access to content — is difficult to compete against. As traditional broadcasters, cable operators, and other streaming entrants expand their own offerings, Webflix risks losing bargaining leverage with content suppliers and may be forced into less favorable licensing or pricing terms to retain members.

Threat 2 — Dependence on third-party rights holders exposes Webflix to content loss, cost inflation, and litigation.
Webflix's ability to keep programming on its platform depends on securing and renewing licenses from studios, content producers, and collection management organizations ("CMOs"), and the 10-K states plainly that if these parties are unwilling to license content on acceptable terms, the Company's costs could rise or its content could be withdrawn on short notice. Because studios are increasingly building their own competing streaming services, they have a growing incentive to pull content rather than license it out, which directly threatens Webflix's catalog depth and negotiating position.
